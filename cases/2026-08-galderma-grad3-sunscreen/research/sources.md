# Research sources — India sunscreen category, competitors, consumer behaviour

All figures below are secondary research, gathered for this case and not
verified against Galderma's own internal data. Where sources disagree
(they do, materially, on market size), both are cited rather than picking
the more convenient number.

## Market size and growth

- **India sun care market** estimated at roughly **USD 481–543 million in
  2024**, with published CAGR estimates ranging **6–9%** through 2030-2033
  depending on the research firm's scope (some include only facial
  sunscreen, others include body/spray/stick formats and men's grooming
  sun products). Treat any single-point figure in this deck as
  directional, not precise.
- **Sunscreen stick format is the fastest-growing sub-format**, cited at
  roughly **10.8% CAGR**, outpacing the category average — consistent with
  the case's own note that new formats (sticks, gels, mists, cushions) are
  where category energy is concentrated.
- Grand View Research and IMARC-style category reports (India sun care /
  sunscreen market reports, 2024-2025 vintage) are the source class for
  the sizing above; both firms publish India-specific sun-care sizing with
  format and channel breakouts.
- Grand View Research. (2024-2025). *India Sun Care Market Size, Share &
  Growth Report.* https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/india-sun-care-market-report
- IMARC Group. (2024-2025). *India Sunscreen Market: Industry Trends,
  Share, Size, Growth, Opportunity and Forecast.*
  https://www.imarcgroup.com/india-sunscreen-market

## Competitive landscape by segment

**Mass segment** (drugstore/general trade, ₹150-400 range):
- Lakmé Sun Expert, Neutrogena, Lotus Herbals, Nivea — legacy FMCG/personal
  care players with the widest general-trade distribution but largely
  unchanged formats (creams/lotions) relative to newer entrants.

**Indie-derma / D2C-first segment** (₹400-900 range, D2C + Nykaa/Amazon +
quick commerce led):
- **The Derma Co.** — ingredient-led marketing (SPF+PA claims foregrounded),
  strong on Nykaa and quick commerce.
- **Dr. Sheth's** — dermatologist-founder positioning, ingredient
  transparency as core brand claim.
- **Dot & Key** — sensorial/format innovation (gel, mist formats),
  strong social-first marketing to the 20s cohort.
- **Minimalist** — full-transparency ingredient-percentage labeling,
  built brand equity specifically on "no white cast" and formulation
  literacy content.
- **Re'equil** — one of the earlier Indian derma-first sunscreen brands,
  broad SPF/PA range, positioned on "for Indian skin."
- **Foxtale, Aqualogica** — newer D2C entrants competing on hybrid
  sunscreen-moisturizer/primer formats and youth-skewed content marketing.

**Premium / derma-cosmetic segment** (₹1,000+, dermatologist-recommended,
often pharmacy or premium-beauty-retail distribution):
- **La Roche-Posay Anthelios** (L'Oréal) — the direct international
  benchmark for "dermatologist-trusted heritage brand extending into
  sensorial modern formats," the same strategic position Galderma is
  being asked to build for Cetaphil/Biluma.
- Galderma's own Cetaphil and Biluma sit here today, but with a narrower
  format range (light gel; lotion) than La Roche-Posay's multi-format
  Anthelios line (fluid, gel, stick, spray, tinted).

**White space read:** the indie-derma segment has won the "ingredient
transparency + format innovation" narrative with 25-35 year olds despite
having far less actual dermatological R&D pedigree than Galderma. Galderma
has the credibility indie brands are still building, but not yet the
format range or sensorial/content presence they've built it with.

## Consumer barriers — white cast as the central adherence problem

- The single most-cited reason for low/inconsistent sunscreen reapplication
  among Indian consumers, across category and dermatology sources, is
  **visible white cast on medium-to-deep skin tones** — a formulation
  issue specific to mineral/hybrid filters not optimized for higher-melanin
  skin, not a purely preference-driven objection.
- A 2020 peer-reviewed dermatology review (*Journal of the American
  Academy of Dermatology*) on sunscreen photoprotection and formulation
  across skin types is the academic anchor for why white cast
  disproportionately affects deeper skin tones and why "no white cast" has
  become a headline claim (Biluma already makes this claim in-market,
  per the case brief) rather than a marketing nicety.
- Zhou, S. et al. or equivalent authors. (2020). *Sunscreens and
  photoprotection.* Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology —
  cited here as the category-standard academic reference for
  formulation-driven cast and tolerability issues across skin tones;
  specific claims Galderma makes on-pack should be re-verified against a
  current, full citation before publication.
- Secondary consumer-behaviour barriers commonly cited alongside white
  cast: greasy/heavy sensorial feel in humid weather, reapplication being
  "forgotten" because of the previous two frictions, and low
  category education on SPF/PA numbers translating to purchase confusion
  (consumers buying on brand/format recognition more than on protection
  specs).

## Distribution — quick commerce as a structural shift for this category

- Quick commerce (Blinkit, Zepto, Swiggy Instamart) beauty & personal care
  GMV is approaching a **~USD 1 billion annualized run-rate**, growing
  roughly **90% year-over-year** as of 2025 reporting — one of the
  fastest-growing channels in Indian retail.
- Sunscreen is specifically named in quick-commerce category breakdowns as
  a **high-frequency replenishment and impulse-repurchase category** —
  consumers reorder small-format sunscreen the way they reorder daily
  personal-care staples, which rewards smaller pack sizes and frequent
  reformulation/limited-edition drops over single large-format SKUs.
- Mohan, M. (2025). *State of Indian quick commerce market 2025 —
  Blinkit, Zepto, Big Basket, Swiggy and Zomato.*
  https://mukundmohan.blog/2025/05/13/state-of-indian-quick-commerce-market-2025-blinkit-zepto-big-basket-swiggy-and-zomato/

## What this means for the strategy (used in analysis.md)

1. White cast is not a niche complaint — it's the primary lever standing
   between Galderma's existing dermatological credibility and actual daily
   adherence in the 25-35 cohort, and Biluma already has a live claim to
   build the portfolio's format strategy around.
2. The competitive threat isn't mass players — it's indie-derma brands
   winning the "trust + format + content" narrative with less R&D
   pedigree than Galderma actually has. The white space is a brand that
   can out-credential the indies (Galderma can) while out-format them
   (Galderma currently cannot, and needs to).
3. Quick commerce isn't just a channel to list on — it structurally favors
   smaller, more frequent-repurchase pack formats and faster
   claim/format iteration than Galderma's current two-SKU portfolio
   supports.
