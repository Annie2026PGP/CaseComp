01

The site — and why this one

This is the walk from the library to class — not a destination anyone chooses, just the corridor every student is already forced through, several times a day, for two years. Today it offers nothing: no seat, no shade worth sitting in, no reason to be five minutes late for anything.

We picked it over a courtyard or a hostel lawn on purpose. A destination space is somewhere students decide to go. This is somewhere they already are — which means a good idea here compounds every day instead of waiting to be discovered.

Note on the brief: we're proposing this space from general knowledge of the campus, not a verified site visit — the exact stretch, dimensions and any electrical or structural constraints should be confirmed on the ground before this goes further.

The redesign — everyday joy, five moves

“The Interval.”

/ the gap between classes — and the pause it's built for /

1Charging + social nookPower, bright, made for a quick loud catch-up between sessions.
2The Memory WallOne long chalkboard. Left blank on purpose — whatever ends up on it is written by students, not us.
3Modular seatingMovable blocks and a swing bench — a group of 2 or 12 both fit without asking anyone.
4Quiet / net nookShaded hammock seating for the five minutes you need to not talk to anyone.
5Pergola + evening lightsShade by day. On a timer, the same corridor becomes a place to be at 9pm.

04

What outlives us

A bench doesn't get remembered. A wall you were allowed to write on does.

The one ritual we're proposing: in the last week of the programme, each outgoing batch leaves a message on the Memory Wall. It's photographed and archived — then the wall is wiped for the batch arriving in July. Nothing else about the space needs a maintenance plan for this to work.

Movable furniture means the campus's own committees can rebuild the space for a fest stall, a farewell, an outdoor case discussion — without asking anyone's permission. The space keeps adapting to campus life instead of the other way round.

General notes — the three questions, straight answers

01
Which space, and why?
The library-to-academic-block walkway — not a destination students choose, but the corridor they're already in, more often than anywhere else on campus.
02
How does it create everyday joy?
Five small moves — modular seating, a blank chalk wall, a charging nook, a quiet nook, and evening lighting — so the same stretch has a reason to be used at 8am, midday, 3pm and 9pm alike.
03
How does it last?
The Memory Wall carries itself forward with almost no upkeep — each batch inherits the last one's marks and adds its own. The one ritual (a final message before graduation) is what turns furniture into something students actually remember two, five, ten years on.
