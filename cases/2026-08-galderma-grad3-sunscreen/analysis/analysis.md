# Analysis — a winning Galderma sunscreen portfolio for India's 25-35 cohort

Grounded in `research/sources.md`. All market/consumer claims are cited
secondary research, not Galderma-internal data — see that file's caveat.

## 1. The consumer — three jobs, one shared blocker

Rather than one persona, the 25-35 Indian sunscreen buyer is doing three
different jobs depending on occasion. Treating them as one persona is
exactly the kind of undifferentiated portfolio thinking the case is asking
us to move past.

**Persona A — "The Commuter."** Applies sunscreen as a defensive, daily
habit before stepping outside for work/commute. Wants: fast absorption, no
retouching mid-day, doesn't want to think about it again till tomorrow.
Primary barrier: reapplication friction (greasy feel + white cast make a
second application at 2pm socially and physically unappealing, so it just
doesn't happen).

**Persona B — "The Skintok Regular."** Treats sunscreen as step 5-7 of a
visible skincare routine, filmed or at least influenced by content she
watches. Wants: a product that behaves well *under makeup or alone on
camera*, ingredient-deck literacy (she can name what "niacinamide" or
"zinc oxide" does), and a pack she's not embarrassed to have on a shelf in
a reel. Primary barrier: today's Cetaphil/Biluma formats read as
"medicinal," not "routine-worthy" — dermatological credibility is an
asset she wants but the current packaging/format doesn't signal it.

**Persona C — "The Occasional Buyer."** Only reaches for sunscreen for
specific high-exposure occasions — a beach trip, a wedding, a long
outdoor day. Wants: strong, legible protection claims (SPF/PA numbers she
trusts), doesn't want to commit to a daily-use price point or a large
pack she won't finish. Primary barrier: category education gap — buys on
brand recall and pack size more than on formulation understanding.

**The blocker that unites all three:** white cast. It is the single most
cited reason Indian consumers under-apply or don't reapply sunscreen
(`research/sources.md`), and it hits every persona differently — the
Commuter skips reapplication, the Skintok Regular won't put it on camera,
the Occasional Buyer associates "sunscreen" with a bad past experience
that keeps her buying infrequently. A portfolio strategy that fixes white
cast across formats is not a feature improvement; it is the single
highest-leverage move available to Galderma in this category.

## 2. Journey and unmet needs

| Stage | What happens today | Unmet need |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Social content (dermatologist creators, skinfluencers) drives awareness far more than in-store browsing | A visible, credible content presence — Galderma's dermatological R&D story isn't showing up where discovery happens |
| Trial | Small-format trial mostly via quick commerce or Nykaa/Amazon sample kits | Cetaphil/Biluma pack sizes and price points aren't optimized for low-commitment trial |
| Daily use | Applied once in the morning; reapplication drop-off is severe | Format needs to disappear into a routine — no white cast, no greasy residue, no visible "sunscreen smell" |
| Repeat | Reordered on quick commerce as a replenishment SKU, or abandoned after one bad-fit tube | Needs to win the *second* purchase, not just the first — which is a formulation and format bet, not a marketing one |

## 3. Competitive landscape — 2x2 and the white space

Two axes matter most for this cohort: **credibility** (does the brand
have real dermatological weight, vs. is it a marketing-led beauty brand)
and **format/sensorial modernity** (does it feel like 2026's skincare, vs.
a legacy pharmacy product).

```
                    HIGH FORMAT / SENSORIAL MODERNITY
                              |
   Dot & Key, Foxtale,        |        La Roche-Posay Anthelios
   Aqualogica, Minimalist     |        (multi-format: fluid/gel/
   (content-led, format-      |        stick/spray/tinted, derma-
   forward, but limited       |        cosmetic distribution)
   derm R&D pedigree)         |
   ---------------------------+---------------------------
   Lakmé, Neutrogena, Nivea   |        Cetaphil, Biluma
   (mass, legacy formats,     |        (real derm credibility,
   little format innovation)  |        narrow 2-SKU format range —
                              |        THE GAP)
                    LOW FORMAT / SENSORIAL MODERNITY
        LOW CREDIBILITY  <----------->  HIGH CREDIBILITY
```

**The white space:** the top-right quadrant — high dermatological
credibility *and* high format/sensorial modernity — is where La
Roche-Posay operates internationally but has not fully localized for
Indian skin tones and climate. No brand currently owns "real derm science,
formatted and marketed like the indie brands India's 25-35s already
trust." Indie-derma brands (top-left) have won the narrative with less
R&D behind them; Cetaphil and Biluma (bottom-right) have the R&D but not
the format range or content presence to win the narrative back.

**Galderma's source of competitive advantage:** it doesn't have to build
credibility from zero, the way every indie D2C brand has had to. It has
to translate credibility it already owns into the formats, claims, and
channels this cohort actually uses to decide. That translation gap — not
a science gap — is the strategic problem this portfolio needs to solve.

## 4. What this rules in and out

- **Ruled in:** expanding format range (gel, stick, mist/spray, tinted)
  under both Cetaphil and Biluma; making "no white cast" a portfolio-wide
  engineering standard, not a single-SKU claim; smaller trial-size SKUs
  built for quick-commerce discovery; content/education presence that
  makes dermatological credibility legible to a skincare-literate
  audience instead of implicit.
- **Ruled out:** a third, brand-new sub-brand — the case explicitly asks
  the portfolio to leverage and complement the *existing* brand
  portfolio, and Cetaphil/Biluma already carry differentiated
  positioning (Cetaphil = sensitive-skin/dermatologist-recommended
  mass-premium; Biluma = brightening/no-white-cast active-ingredient
  premium) that a new name would dilute rather than sharpen.
- **Ruled out:** competing on price against mass players — Galderma's
  right to win is credibility + format, not shelf price; competing there
  cedes the advantage it actually has.

## 5. The portfolio strategy (3-5 years)

**Portfolio idea in one line:** Cetaphil and Biluma stop being two
sunscreens and become two *systems* — each with a format range built
around a distinct job, both engineered from day one to eliminate white
cast, so Galderma's derm credibility finally shows up in a shape this
cohort already wants to buy.

### Portfolio hierarchy — role of each brand and tier

| Brand | Role | Target job | Segment position |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cetaphil Sun** | The daily-wearable system — disappears into a routine, for Persona A (Commuter) and as the reliable base layer for Persona B | Protect without being noticed: fast-absorbing, no white cast, no re-thinking | Mass-premium, widest distribution |
| **Biluma Sun** | The visible-actives system — protection *plus* a skin-goal claim (brightening, barrier, anti-pollution), for Persona B and Persona C | Protect *and* show a result — the SPF that also does something you can point to | Premium derma-cosmetic, narrower/higher-margin distribution |

This mirrors a hierarchy the case's own benchmark (La Roche-Posay) uses —
a broad, dermatologically-trusted daily line plus a narrower actives-led
premium line — but built on brands Galderma already owns rather than a
new one.

### Format architecture (rolled out in phases, not all at once)

**Phase 1 (Year 1) — fix the core, plug the format gap:**
- Reformulate the existing Cetaphil Light Gel and Biluma Sunscreen to a
  verified, testable "no white cast on medium-to-deep tones" standard —
  the single highest-leverage move identified in Section 1.
- Launch **Cetaphil Sun Stick SPF50+** — matches the fastest-growing
  sub-format (~10.8% CAGR, `research/sources.md`), purse/gym-bag-sized,
  designed explicitly as the reapplication product (solves the Commuter's
  #1 drop-off point: nobody reapplies a tube at their desk, everyone will
  swipe a stick).
- Launch **Biluma Sun Mist SPF50+** — a spray/mist format for
  over-makeup reapplication, targeted at Persona B, who currently has no
  reapplication option that doesn't ruin her makeup.

**Phase 2 (Years 2-3) — build the actives-led premium tier:**
- **Biluma Sun Tinted SPF50+** — sunscreen + light coverage in one step,
  the highest-demand crossover format among skincare-literate 25-35s who
  want to cut a step, not add one.
- **Cetaphil Sun Gel-Cream SPF50+** — a mid-weight option between the
  existing Light Gel and a heavier cream, for combination-skin users in
  humid climates who found the Light Gel too light and a standard cream
  too heavy.

**Phase 3 (Years 3-5) — defend and extend:**
- Small-format trial/travel SKUs (15-20ml) across the winning formats
  from Phases 1-2, purpose-built for quick-commerce trial economics.
- Evaluate a **Cetaphil Sun Kids/Family** line only if Phase 1-2 data
  shows household repeat-buying — extension option, not a Year 1 bet.

### SPF architecture

Standardize on **SPF50+ PA++++ across the entire portfolio.** India's UV
index and the case's own framing (hot, humid, high sun-exposure climate)
make a lower-SPF "everyday" tier a false economy — it would ask the
consumer to make a protection trade-off Galderma doesn't need to offer.
Differentiate by **format and finish**, not by protection level: this
keeps the claims simple (every Galderma sunscreen is "the good one") and
avoids fragmenting a still-low-education category with a confusing SPF
ladder, directly addressing the purchase-confusion barrier in Section 2.

### Benefit claims and differentiating ingredients

- **Portfolio-wide baseline claim:** "No White Cast" — currently a
  Biluma-only claim; extended across the whole portfolio as the
  non-negotiable table stakes this analysis identifies as the actual
  unlock.
- **Cetaphil Sun:** "Dermatologist-recommended, all-day comfort" — Vitamin
  E (already in Cetaphil's formulation) retained as the sensitive-skin
  anchor claim; add a lightweight/non-greasy claim tied to the new
  gel-cream and stick formats.
- **Biluma Sun:** "Protection + Visible Skin Goal" — retain and lead with
  Lactic Acid + Hyaluronic Acid (already Biluma's claim), extend with a
  brightening/anti-pollution claim on the tinted variant to compete
  directly with Dot & Key/Minimalist-style ingredient-led marketing, but
  with Galderma's actual dermatological substantiation behind it —
  ingredient decks and clinical-style claim support are a real advantage
  indie brands can't fully match.

### Pack formats, sizes, and pricing strategy

| Format | Size | Indicative price (₹) | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cetaphil Sun Stick | 15-20g | 350-450 | Reapplication / trial / travel |
| Cetaphil Sun Gel-Cream | 50g | 550-650 | Core daily-use SKU |
| Biluma Sun Mist | 60-75ml | 650-750 | Reapplication over makeup |
| Biluma Sun Tinted | 30-40g | 750-900 | Routine step-cutting, premium |
| Trial/travel sizes (Phase 3) | 15-20ml | 150-250 | Quick-commerce trial |

Pricing keeps Cetaphil in mass-premium territory (competitive with The
Derma Co./Re'equil) and Biluma in derma-cosmetic premium territory
(approaching, not matching, La Roche-Posay Anthelios import pricing) —
preserving the two-tier hierarchy rather than collapsing both brands into
one price band.

## 6. Go-to-market

**Channels:** lead with quick commerce and Nykaa/Amazon for trial-size and
reapplication-format SKUs (matches how this cohort actually discovers and
repurchases sunscreen, per Section 3 of `research/sources.md`); use
pharmacy and dermatologist-clinic channels to reinforce Cetaphil/Biluma's
credibility advantage at the point where it matters most — the moment a
consumer is deciding whether to trust an ingredient claim.

**Territories:** prioritize metro and Tier-1 markets in Year 1 (where the
25-35 skincare-literate cohort and quick-commerce penetration are both
highest), expand to Tier-2 with the mass-tier Cetaphil formats in Years
2-3 once the format range is proven.

**Partnerships:** dermatologist-creator partnerships (not generic beauty
influencers) to carry the credibility claim into the content-first
discovery journey identified in Section 2 — this is where Galderma's real
asset (actual dermatologists on staff) is a structural advantage no indie
D2C brand can fully replicate.

**Trial, adoption, repeat:**
- *Trial:* small-format SKUs on quick commerce, priced for one-time
  low-commitment purchase.
- *Adoption:* the reapplication-format SKUs (stick, mist) are the
  adoption wedge — once white cast and reapplication friction are solved,
  daily use follows naturally.
- *Repeat:* quick-commerce subscribe/reorder mechanics on the exact SKU a
  consumer already validated works for them, plus a portfolio "graduate"
  path (Cetaphil user trades up to Biluma's actives-led tier as her
  skincare routine matures) that keeps repeat purchase inside the
  Galderma portfolio instead of losing her to an indie brand at the
  moment she wants "more."

## 7. Risks

- **Reformulation risk:** the "no white cast" standard is a real R&D
  commitment, not a marketing claim — this analysis assumes it's
  achievable within the phase timeline but doesn't attempt the
  formulation science itself.
- **Cannibalization within the portfolio:** Cetaphil's new gel-cream and
  Biluma's existing lotion could overlap; pricing and claims need to stay
  clearly differentiated by finish and skin-goal, not just by brand name.
- **Distribution execution risk:** winning quick commerce requires
  operational readiness (fulfillment, reorder mechanics) that is outside
  this analysis's scope and would need validation with Galderma's actual
  channel partners.
- Figures underpinning market sizing and channel growth are secondary
  research with meaningful variance across sources (see
  `research/sources.md`) — treat as directional, re-verify before
  committing budget.
