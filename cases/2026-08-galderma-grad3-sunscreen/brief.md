## About Galderma

Founded 1981. Global leader in dermatology, self-described as "the only
truly scaled, pure-play dermatology company." Three segments: Injectable
Aesthetics, Dermatological Skincare, Therapeutic Dermatology. Portfolio of
premium, science-backed, dermatologist-trusted brands.

## Brands named in the case

- **Cetaphil Sun SPF50+ Light Gel** — Very high protection, sensitive
  skin, broad spectrum, very water resistant, Vitamin E.
- **Biluma Sunscreen SPF50+ PA++++** — Broad spectrum UVA+UVB, "Advantage
  of Lactic Acid & Hyaluronic Acid," No White Cast, dermatologically
  tested.

## The Indian sunscreen opportunity (as stated in the case)

- Indian sun care market growing rapidly: rising skin-health awareness,
  evolving beauty aspirations among younger consumers, rising disposable
  income, social media influence.
- Category is highly dynamic, with continuous innovation via:
  - New formats: serums, gels, sticks, mists, cushions
  - Enhanced benefit claims: anti-pollution, brightening, oil control,
    skin barrier support
  - Superior sensorial experiences designed for Indian weather conditions
  - Premium, portable, aesthetically appealing packaging

## The problem

Despite strong dermatological credentials, Galderma's sunscreen portfolio
is limited to Cetaphil and Biluma. The existing offering delivers trusted
protection, but its format and sensorial experience may not fully meet
the evolving needs of young Indian consumers, especially in hot and humid
climates.

## The strategic question

**How can Galderma build a winning sunscreen portfolio for India's next
generation of consumers while leveraging its existing brand heritage?**

## The challenge

Your team is appointed strategic consultants to Galderma India. Develop
a future-ready sunscreen portfolio strategy for Indian consumers aged
**25–35**, across Galderma's existing brand portfolio. Recommend a
comprehensive portfolio strategy that lets Galderma win in this
fast-evolving category.

### Key consideration 1 — Consumer & category deep dive

Build a holistic understanding of the Indian sunscreen consumer (25–35):
- Category awareness, attitudes, motivations, and barriers
- Consumer journey, usage occasions, routines, and unmet needs
- Brand landscape, loyalty dynamics, and drivers of choice and switching
- Product, format, benefit-claim, and packaging preferences

### Key consideration 2 — Competitive landscape assessment

Analyse competition across **mass, premium, and derma-cosmetic**
segments. Identify category white spaces and sources of competitive
advantage.

## Expected deliverables

1. A **3–5 year portfolio strategy** including:
   - Recommended product formats
   - SPF architecture
   - Key benefit claims
   - Differentiating ingredients and formulations
   - Pack formats and sizes
   - Pricing strategy
   - A clear portfolio hierarchy defining the role of each SKU
   - The portfolio must both **leverage and complement** the existing
     skincare range.
2. **Go-to-market:** key communication channels and territories, key
   retail/e-commerce channels and partnerships, and strategies to drive
   trial, adoption, and repeat usage.
