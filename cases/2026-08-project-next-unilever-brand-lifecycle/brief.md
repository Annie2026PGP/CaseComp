## About Unilever

Unilever has spent more than a century making products people reach for
without thinking. Around 3.7 billion people use its products on any given
day — a shower in Jakarta, a kitchen in São Paulo, a laundry basket in
Manchester, a bathroom shelf in Mumbai: somewhere in each there is usually
a Unilever brand, whether Dove, Rexona, Domestos, Knorr, Lakmé, or Axe.
Unilever operates across four Business Groups: Beauty & Wellbeing,
Personal Care, Home Care, Nutrition. Behind the wellness products, snacks,
and beverages is something powerful: technology and data transforming
Unilever as an AI-Age organisation, driven by Global Digital & Technology
(GDT) — a global function that serves as the digital backbone for Unilever
and plays a key role in its strategy.

## Background

Deep into stoppage time, with the match still level, the fourth official
lifts his board to signal six more minutes. As his arms go up, the cameras
catch something in the one spot on a football pitch no advertiser had ever
claimed: tucked beneath his arm, a Rexona logo. It lasts barely three
seconds, yet by the final whistle fans have found it for themselves,
frozen the frame, and turned it into the meme of the tournament. For a
Brand Manager of Rexona, this isn't just a viral clip. It is the ultimate
market signal and opportunity. On the sweat-soaked, high-pressure arm of
the one official who cannot afford to lose his cool, the brand is not
interrupting the moment. It is the moment — serving as living proof of our
tagline: "It won't EVER let you down". But here is the immediate
challenge.

The conventional approach to capturing cultural moments lacks agility.
Between agency briefs, sentiment testing, full-scale shoots, and manual
localization across multiple formats, campaign momentum is lost. By the
time the content hits the market, the viral opportunity is dead.

While startups chase trends in hours, Unilever brand managers process
large volumes of collected data. Despite having an arsenal of advanced
technical capabilities, such as social listening and web scraping, these
functions operate independently. By the time managers manually synthesize
these scattered insights, the window of opportunity has closed. The
challenge is not simply to build a unified dashboard, but to fundamentally
transform how we synchronize and act on consumer intelligence across a
brand ecosystem.

## The Challenge

The modern marketing ecosystem demands an unprecedented volume of content
and real-time decision-making. Yet, brand managers find themselves
increasingly overwhelmed by fragmented data, disconnected dashboards, and
endless asset requests. While isolated AI experiments exist across various
departments, the core processes of brand management have yet to evolve at
the same pace.

Recognizing this breaking point, imagine a forward-thinking marketing
manager posing a critical challenge to their team: "We don't just need
another list of standalone generative AI features," they might argue.
"Our competitors will soon buy access to the exact same foundational
models we use. The real question is how we fundamentally rewire our
processes. Show me the system that makes our brand teams invincible."

## The Ask

Imagine you have been hired as the Product Manager for Project NEXT. Your
mission is to reimagine how a global consumer goods company builds and
manages brands from the ground up. If we were founded today in an AI-first
world, how would you architect the entire brand lifecycle?

As a Product Manager, your task is to:

1. Redefine [the] digital landscape [applied] to the end-to-end brand
   management lifecycle and identify where AI can create value.
2. Propose a portfolio of AI-powered products that could support brand
   teams across this lifecycle.
3. Prioritize the most impactful opportunities and explain your rationale.
4. Select one such opportunity/product and design it in detail, covering:
   - User journey
   - Product capabilities
   - AI technologies and agents
   - Architecture and governance
   - Human oversight
   - Costs and implementation roadmap
   - Demonstrate your vision through prototypes, mockups, agents, or live
     demonstrations.

The ask is not simply to propose new foundational models, but to
recommend meaningful changes to the process.

## Submission Guidelines

Submit the proposal in **three slides**:

1. Synopsis and analysis of the problem statement (based on your
   understanding).
2. Proposed portfolio using relevant technology, with product and area of
   solution.
3. Product in depth, with cost estimates and build timelines, etc.

**Notes:**
- Partnerships with startups or third-party tech providers may be
  proposed.
- Real-world examples, mock-ups, or prototypes/functional agent
  demonstration are required to strengthen the pitch. **Submissions
  without a prototype will not be considered.**

## Working interpretation / delivery plan

- Cover + 3 required content slides (per submission guidelines above) +
  appendix (sources/assumptions, doesn't count against the 3-slide limit).
- Portfolio of AI products, all built on a shared backbone (the PULSE
  Signal Graph + Brand DNAi orchestration layer designed earlier this
  session), prioritized by dependency + time-to-value.

  (published as an Artifact), since a live-data functional agent
  connected to real Unilever systems is out of scope for this exercise —
  documented as an illustrative UI/flow prototype, not a production
  system, consistent with the "mock-ups, or prototypes" option the
  guidelines explicitly allow.
