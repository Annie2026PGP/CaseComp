## Brand: Kissan

India's No. 1 ketchup brand and No. 1 jams/preserves brand by retail value
(HUL). Portfolio: ketchup, jam, squash, peanut butter. 90+ years in Indian
kitchens. Brand line: "Taste Jo Chatt Kar Jao."

## The tension the case wants you to sit with

Kissan is category leader in ketchup and jam but narrow beyond them.
Challenger brands (Veeba, Ching's named explicitly) haven't tried to
out-ketchup Kissan — they've built themselves as **Masterbrands spanning
sauces, dips, mayo and dressings**, so trust in one product extends to the
next ten. The case explicitly asks: *why doesn't Kissan just reinvent
ketchup/jam and call it a day?* — and explicitly names the intended
direction in its own "why Kissan can win" section: extend authority into
**chutneys and other untapped white spaces**, not just refresh the core.

The moodboard ("CHATORAS") the case ships alongside this text is itself a
brief: ketchup on dosa, chutney on pizza, double/triple dipping, "kiss the
rules goodbye" — a portrait of borderless, rule-breaking dipping behavior
across cuisines and formats.

## Global/local context given in the case (Part 1)

- 2026 findings (attributed to Mintel, Innova Market Insights, Tastewise,
  Datassential): variety is the new health; heritage is becoming relevant
  again; flavours have no borders; experience matters as much as taste.
  Hot & spicy sauces are the fastest-growing sauce category globally,
  8%+ CAGR.
- Indian condiment culture (chutney, pickle, achaar) is being *rediscovered*
  by the same generation experimenting with global flavours — not a
  contradiction, both at once.
- Quick commerce has collapsed the gap between seeing a food trend and
  having it delivered (craving-to-kitchen in ~15 minutes).
- Today's kitchen: multigenerational influence, digitally-inspired cooking,
  regional pride, more experimentation, online discovery before in-store.
- Two player archetypes: **Legacy brands** (trusted, familiar, slower to
  innovate, one hero category) vs. **Challenger brands** (fast, culturally
  relevant, experimental, Masterbrand across the shelf).
- Kissan's stated strengths: **Scale** (distribution across modern/general/
  emerging trade), **Trust** (deep penetration built over decades),
  **Credibility** (quality ingredients, farmer/agriculture roots).

## Your mandate

Identify Kissan's next growth opportunity and build a compelling product
mix around it: a genuine new product idea, backed by real consumer
insighting, approached like a HUL Brand Manager — define the opportunity,
shape the product proposition, build a go-to-market plan for how people
*discover* it, before anyone gets to advertising.

### Task 1 — Discover the consumer (mandatory primary research)

Talk to **20 people**, across **at least two generations** and ideally
**more than one city tier**. Sit with them while they cook. Open a fridge
with permission. Explicitly *not* a Google Form. Explore: what drives
experimentation; which food rituals are non-negotiable; where consumers
seek convenience; what flavours/experiences are missing; how consumers
discover new food brands and what triggers first trial; what makes a
brand read as "new/exciting" vs. "old/safe" — and what specifically (look,
sound, behaviour) creates that read.

### Task 2 — Define the opportunity (one slide)

- The white space, and why it exists **now** (not two years ago, not two
  years from now).
- Why Kissan is uniquely positioned to win here — different from simply
  improving the existing ketchup/jam business.
- The Big Idea, in one line.
- The consumer insight underneath, framed as something the consumer
  *already believes or feels* that the idea directly speaks to.

Opportunity must: address a genuine need, be relevant today, be hard for
competitors to replicate, leverage Kissan's actual strengths, and go
beyond a line extension or incremental variant.

### Task 3 — Build it out

- **Product** — format, flavour, core ingredients, genuine point of
  difference from what's already on the shelf.
- **Packaging** — pack look/feel; the one visual cue that says "new
  Kissan," not "old Kissan."
- **Proposition** — beyond this one product: how should Kissan look,
  sound and behave differently; price point and why, for this consumer,
  at this occasion.
- **Price** — the pricing logic.

## Submission requirements (Stage 1)

1. A **60-second elevator pitch video**.
2. **Two supporting slides**, on HUL's exact templates:
   - **Slide 1 — Consumer Job:** persona definition → opportunity →
     "When I... / I want to... / So that..." job-to-be-done statement.
   - **Slide 2 — Concept Card (consumer-facing pitch):** compelling
     functional *and* emotional benefit; built on an insight that is
     **never explicitly written on the card**; bold, creative language;
     crisp copy at **≤80 words**; one bold image (pack or talent). This
     card is shown to real consumers for feedback, so it has to stand
     alone and read clean.

If shortlisted to the campus top 5: build the full business case and
pitch live to the HUL jury, "the same way a brand team would pitch a new
launch to leadership for sign-off." Not required at this stage.

## How this was actually approached

**Task 1 cannot be fabricated.** The case is explicit that this is the
part that "will make or break your idea," and a jury reading real
2026-dated consumer quotes will recognize invented ones. Rather than
inventing 20 fake interviews, this repo contains:

- `research/discovery-guide.md` — a ready-to-run interview script,
  screening quotas, and a synthesis template mapped to the case's six
  exploration questions, so the actual team can execute Task 1 quickly
  and well.
- A white-space hypothesis and Big Idea (`analysis/analysis.md`) built
  from **secondary research** (market sizing, competitor strategy,
  category trend data — all cited) rather than primary interviews. This
  is explicitly a hypothesis to pressure-test against the real 20
  interviews, not a substitute for them — and the deck says so.
