# Project NEXT — Reimagining the AI-First Brand Lifecycle

- **Received:** 2026-08-06
- **Due:** not specified
- **Team:** Ranjana Mainani

## Prompt

Deep into stoppage time, with the match still level, the fourth official
lifts his board to signal six more minutes. As his arms go up, the cameras
catch something in the one spot on a football pitch no advertiser had ever
claimed: tucked beneath his arm, a Rexona logo. It lasts barely three
seconds, yet by the final whistle fans have found it for themselves, frozen
the frame, and turned it into the meme of the tournament.

For a Brand Manager of Rexona, this isn't just a viral clip. It is the
ultimate market signal and opportunity. On the sweat-soaked, high-pressure
arm of the one official who cannot afford to lose his cool, the brand is
not interrupting the moment. It is the moment — serving as living proof of
our tagline: "It won't EVER let you down."

But here is the immediate challenge. The conventional approach to
capturing cultural moments lacks agility. Between agency briefs, sentiment
testing, full-scale shoots, and manual localization across multiple
formats, campaign momentum is lost. By the time the content hits the
market, the viral opportunity is dead.

While startups chase trends in hours, Unilever brand managers process
large volumes of collected data. Despite having an arsenal of advanced
technical capabilities, such as social listening and web scraping, these
functions operate independently. By the time managers manually synthesize
these scattered insights, the window of opportunity has closed. The
challenge is not simply to build a unified dashboard, but to fundamentally
transform how we synchronize and act on consumer intelligence across a
brand ecosystem.

The modern marketing ecosystem demands an unprecedented volume of content
and real-time decision-making. Yet, brand managers find themselves
increasingly overwhelmed by fragmented data, disconnected dashboards, and
endless asset requests. While isolated AI experiments exist across various
departments, the core processes of brand management have yet to evolve at
the same pace.

Recognizing this breaking point, imagine a forward-thinking marketing
manager posing a critical challenge to their team: "We don't just need
another list of standalone generative AI features," they might argue. "Our
competitors will soon buy access to the exact same foundational models we
use. The real question is how we fundamentally rewire our processes. Show
me the system that makes our brand teams invincible."

**Your mission (as Product Manager for Project NEXT):** reimagine how a
global consumer goods company builds and manages brands from the ground
up. If we were founded today in an AI-first world, how would you
architect the entire brand lifecycle?

## Working interpretation

This is an open-ended product/system-design case with no dataset and no
stated slide/word limit. Treated as: design and pitch a real system (not
a feature list), grounded in Unilever's actual existing AI capabilities
(Brand DNAi, Desire at Scale, digital-twin product imagery — see
`research/sources.md`), that:

1. Answers the reactive hook (the Rexona moment) with a concrete,
   timed walkthrough of how the system would have shipped a response in
   hours, not weeks.
2. Answers the broader ask ("architect the entire brand lifecycle") by
   showing the same underlying system serving three different clock
   speeds: real-time moments, planned campaigns, and long-cycle brand
   strategy — not just a real-time-marketing bolt-on.
3. Answers the "invincible" / moat question directly: since competitors
   can buy the same foundational models, the defensible asset has to be
   something that compounds over time and can't be bought off the shelf.
