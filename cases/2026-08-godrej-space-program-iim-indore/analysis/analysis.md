# Analysis — The Space Program (IIM Indore)

## The space we chose, and why

**The covered walkway/breezeway connecting the Learning Resource Centre
(library) to the main Academic Block** — the stretch every student on
campus crosses multiple times a day, between classes, before and after
library sessions, for the full two years of the programme.

We chose an archetype rather than a single hyper-specific location, for two
reasons:

1. **Honesty about what we can verify.** This proposal is written without a
   live site visit or verified campus floor plan — general knowledge of IIM
   Indore's campus (hillside site, river-named hostels, a modern glass
   academic block, a library, an amphitheater) informs the tone, but exact
   building names, adjacencies and dimensions have **not** been confirmed
   against the current campus. The library-to-academic-block connector is
   deliberately the kind of space almost certainly exists in some real,
   walkable form regardless of the exact building names — a team actually on
   campus should confirm the precise stretch and adjust plans/dimensions
   before this goes further.
2. **It fits the brief's own criteria better than a destination space would.**
   A courtyard or hostel lawn is somewhere students choose to go. A
   between-classes corridor is somewhere students are *already forced to be*,
   every single day, at the highest possible frequency of any space on
   campus — which is exactly what makes an "everyday joy" and "lasting
   impact" intervention compound over two years instead of being a one-off
   destination.

Today, by every description in the brief, this is a **pure transit
corridor**: nowhere to sit, nothing to look at, no reason to linger. Students
who want five minutes between a library session and a case class either
stand, sit on the floor, or leave the space entirely — the campus loses a
huge amount of accidental social time to a stretch of pavement.

## The redesign: "The Interval"

Named for the double meaning — the gap between classes, and the pause it's
designed to invite. The redesign has five elements, each aimed at a
different moment of the day rather than one generic "seating area":

1. **Modular, movable seating** — low blocks, bean bags and a swing bench
   instead of fixed benches, so a group of 2 or a group of 12 can both make
   the space their own without asking permission from the furniture.
2. **The Memory Wall** — one long chalkboard-textured wall, intentionally
   left blank at launch. No corporate signage, no fixed content — just
   chalk, left there. What ends up on it (case-cracking diagrams, doodles,
   "3 days to placements," a farewell message from a graduating batch) is
   authored by students, continuously, for as long as the space exists.
3. **Two contrasting nooks** — a high-energy charging/social nook near one
   end (power outlets, bright), and a quiet hammock/reading nook at the
   other (net seating, shaded, phone-optional) — so the same 40 metres of
   corridor serves both "I need five minutes of noise" and "I need five
   minutes of quiet."
4. **A pergola with climbing greenery** — shade against Indore's heat during
   the day; the actual point of building it is what it enables at night.
5. **Evening lighting** — string lights woven through the pergola, on a
   timer, so the same transit corridor that's purely functional at 9am
   becomes a place people choose to be at 9pm: impromptu guitar circles,
   pre-fest hangouts, farewell photos.

## Why it lasts, not just launches

The mechanism that makes this different from "nice furniture that gets
dusty in year three":

- **The Memory Wall is self-renewing by design.** It has no fixed content,
  so there's nothing to maintain except chalk supply — and every incoming
  batch inherits a wall already full of the batch before them, which is
  itself an invitation to add to it rather than start fresh. We recommend
  formalising one ritual moment: outgoing batches leave a wall message in
  their final week, photographed and archived before it's erased for the
  new batch. That single ritual, repeated, is what turns furniture into
  institutional memory.
- **Modularity means student ownership, not fixed corporate branding.**
  Because the furniture is movable, the campus's own event committees can
  reconfigure the space for a specific evening (a fest stall, a farewell
  gathering, an outdoor case discussion) without needing anyone's
  permission or budget — the space adapts to campus life instead of campus
  life working around a fixed installation.
- **Two-zone design means it never runs out of a reason to be used.** A
  single-purpose "hangout corner" gets used for a season and then ignored.
  A corridor that's simultaneously a charging stop, a quiet nook, a chalk
  wall and an evening hangout has at least one relevant use at every hour
  of the day, which is what keeps daily footfall high enough for the space
  to stay part of the routine rather than becoming a novelty.
- **Highest possible touchpoint frequency.** Every student crosses this
  stretch multiple times a day for two years — roughly 700+ crossings per
  student over the programme. No destination space on campus can match that
  frequency, which is the real reason this location was chosen over a more
  "impressive" but less-visited spot.

## What the storyboard needs to do

Per the brief's three required questions, mapped to the panels:

1. *Which space, and why* → opening/before panels + the frequency argument.
2. *Everyday joy* → the day-in-the-life panels (morning / interval / quiet
   afternoon / evening).
3. *Lasting impact* → the Memory Wall ritual panel and the graduation
   hand-off panel, explicitly.

## Open items for the real team to close before submission

- Confirm the actual space, exact dimensions and any structural/electrical
  constraints (pergola anchoring, lighting wiring, ADA/accessibility path
  width) with campus facilities.
- Get a rough materials/budget estimate — this proposal is concept-stage,
  not a costed design brief.
- Decide on final teammate credits for the cover.
